# Sun 22 Nov 2026

**The three that matter:** The Lean and Mathlib communities have certified
Alpöge's Riemann Hypothesis proof as correct after independent kernels replayed
the whole thing. A crafted OpenPGP key double-frees GPGME, which most mail
clients link. ULA says Vulcan's failure was fuel starvation inside the vehicle,
not the BE-4 engines.

## Science + space

### The Riemann Hypothesis proof has been checked, and it holds
Yesterday this was an unverified claim. The Lean and Mathlib communities, with
the Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative endorsing, now certify that Alpöge's
17-million-line library proves exactly Mathlib's `RiemannHypothesis`, plus the
Dirichlet L-function statements that give GRH. All 175,187 modules compile, the
five headline theorems lean on only the three standard axioms, and two
independently written kernels accept the full export.
[source](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/)

### Vulcan starved its own engines
ULA's initial integrated review of the 15 November Amazon Leo 1 loss points at
inadequate fuel supply from the vehicle to the BE-4 engines rather than the
engines themselves. Stage separation happened low and slow; the RL10 upper stage
started nominally and could not make up the deficit. Solid booster performance
was nominal. The FAA approves the final report.
[source](https://blog.ulalaunch.com/blog/vulcan-leo-1-updates)

## Dev + infra

### A long user ID in an OpenPGP key double-frees GPGME
GPGME's status-line reader reallocates its buffer but only writes the new
pointer back to the context at the end of the function, so an early return
leaves a stale pointer that gets freed twice. Exfiltrating data by sending
signed content works; RCE is not ruled out. Update to 2.3.1 — mail clients,
Samba and Flatpak all link this.
[source](https://lists.gnupg.org/pipermail/gnupg-announce/2026q4/000517.html)

### C++26 failed its approval ballot and has been finished a second time
ISO's DIS ballot went non-unanimous for the first time ever, sent back over
contracts. Six days in Búzios later the committee reheard the objections,
declined to remove contracts, adopted Berne's always-enforced label instead, and
revoted 111–9 to reship. A second ballot follows. Two thirds of the 220
attendees were remote after the US ground stop.
[source](https://herbsutter.com/2026/11/21/c26-is-done-again-trip-report-november-2026-iso-c-standards-meeting-buzios-brazil/)

## AI research + releases

### GPT-6.2 scores worse than GPT-6 on SimpleBench
The leaderboard now has GPT-6.2 Astra Pro at 79.2% and Astra at 77.3%, against
86.5% and 83.6% for the GPT-6 versions; GPT-6.2 Sol drops to 69.9% from 82.9%.

### Inkling 2 Mini runs its middle layers three times per token
Thinking Machines' 26.4B-parameter MoE activates 3.4B and loops the middle
layers three times, trading written reasoning for internal passes: 48.2% on
DeepSWE v1.1, ahead of every open-weights model under 100B. Weights are native
NVFP4, ~20GB all up, so it runs offline on a laptop. A variable-loop research
variant ships alongside.
[source](https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/)

## Hardware

### DDR6 breaks the 64-bit DIMM and shrinks the module
JEDEC's preview widens the DIMM bus to 96 bits after three decades, at 12.8 GT/s
rising to 14.4 and beyond — putting a DDR6-14400 DIMM at 172.8GB/s, 2.35x a
DDR5-9200. Max die density doubles to 128 Gbit and the module narrows from 133mm
to 74mm, because sixteen channels of DDR5 no longer physically fit. No SO-DIMM
is mentioned at all.
[source](https://www.servethehome.com/jedec-previews-ddr6-memory-standard-2-35x-more-bandwidth-44-narrower-dimms/)

is "extensively improved", `vf_fruc_vulkan` does NVIDIA optical-flow frame rate
