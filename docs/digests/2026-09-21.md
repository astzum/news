# Mon 21 Sep 2026

**The three that matter:** AWS has told customers that anything held only in its Bahrain
region is permanently unrecoverable, and that one of three UAE availability zones is gone
with it — war damage that exceeded what multi-AZ services are built to survive. RSA-896
was factored on 19 September, the second AI-assisted factoring record in seventeen days
and the largest RSA number broken so far. And a researcher has traced how a single
OpenAI cookie, `__obi`, is the only one in the set configured to cross sites, carrying a
ChatGPT account identifier onto advertisers' pages.

## Dev + infra

### AWS has written off an entire region's data
Six months after Iranian drone strikes hit its Middle East datacentres, AWS posted a
Health Dashboard update on 15 September saying it is "unable to restore access to the
resources and data hosted exclusively in this Region" — the Region being Bahrain,
`me-south-1`, all three availability zones. The sentence to sit with is the one before it:
"The damage to our infrastructure spanned multiple Availability Zones and exceeded what
our regional and multi-AZ services are designed to withstand." The UAE region lost
`mec1-az2` the same way; work continues on `mec1-az1` and `mec1-az3`. AWS says most
customers migrated out after the first Bahrain zone was hit in March, before a second
went down in April, and it declined to add anything beyond the dashboard text. The next
update is promised for early 2027. Multi-AZ is a redundancy story, not a disaster-recovery
story, and this is the first time the difference has been settled by something other than
argument: the durability guarantees you buy are scoped to the failure modes the provider
modelled, and a region is a blast radius.
[report](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2026/09/16/aws-says-wartime-damage-means-some-middle-east-cloud-resources-are-gone-for-good/5296830)

### Postgres full-text search got fast by throwing out document IDs
PlanetScale shipped TIN on 16 September, GA on its Postgres and Neki databases. The design
idea is that every other text index assigns its own sequential document numbers and then
pays to map them back to rows; TIN uses the 48-bit `ctid` directly as the document ID, which
kills the mapping lookup, lets it compress postings as a two-level page/offset bitmap, and
lets segments merge without renumbering anything. Benchmarked on an i7i.8xlarge against
ParadeDB 0.25.2, pg_textsearch 1.4.0 and Postgres 18.6's built-in GIN, in a container
capped at 8 vCPU and 32 GB so the index does not simply live in buffers — of those, only
ParadeDB finished every workload. On disjunction queries with 1,000 UPDATEs/sec running
concurrently, TIN served 57x ParadeDB's queries at a p99 36x lower while completing 270,279
updates to ParadeDB's 185,584; pg_textsearch managed 735 updates in ten minutes because
read traffic never released the locks. The catch is the obvious one: there is no mention of
a source release anywhere in the post, so this is a reason to be on PlanetScale, not an
extension you can install.
[announcement](https://planetscale.com/blog/introducing-tin)

## AI research + releases

### RSA-896 has been factored, three weeks after the last record fell
Stephen A. Weis published the two 448-bit primes on 19 September and nothing else — no
method, no compute figures, just the numbers. They check out: multiply them and you get the
RSA-896 challenge number exactly, and both pass Miller-Rabin. He credits Claude; his own bio
says he works at Anthropic, which is worth knowing when reading the claim. What makes this
more than a stunt is the item directly above it in the record books. RSA-260 (862 bits) fell
on 3 September to Eric Lu at Cognition, and that one came with a full writeup: a heavily
modified CADO-NFS with a GPU lattice siever and block Wiedemann solver, built and operated
by Devin across 192 sessions, about 4,900 GPU-days and an estimated $400k of compute at
market rates. Two records in seventeen days, both from small teams pointing an agent at a
sieve implementation rather than organising a volunteer grid. Cognition's own extrapolation
is the useful number to carry: roughly $30M for RSA-1024, with RSA-2048 still about a
billion times harder. Nothing here threatens a 2048-bit key. It does mean the cost curve for
the sizes below it is now being pushed by people who don't need a national lab.
[factors](https://saweis.net/posts/rsa-896.html) ·
[RSA-260 writeup](https://cognition.com/blog/factoring-rsa-260)

### Qwen shipped a much smaller image model and took the licence back
Qwen-Image-2.1's weights landed over the weekend: 7B in the visual generation component
against 20B for last year's Qwen-Image, with mixed-granularity attention and prefix KV cache
reuse doing the work. The genuinely new capability is native RGBA — it generates transparent
images and edits transparent layers directly, rather than being followed by a matting step —
plus editing against up to ten reference images, local edits specified by circling or
painting on the image, and 2048px native output across the usual aspect ratios. The whole
repo is 33 GB in bf16, of which the transformer is 14.2 GB and the text encoder 17.5 GB, so
`enable_model_cpu_offload()` is how this fits on a 24 GB card. Read the licence before you
plan anything on it: Qwen-Image was Apache-2.0, and 2.1 ships under the Qwen Research
License, which grants use "FOR NON-COMMERCIAL PURPOSES ONLY" and defines non-commercial as
research or evaluation, with commercial use requiring a separate agreement. That is a
capability upgrade and a rights downgrade in the same release.
[model card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen-Image-2.1)

## AI industry

### One OpenAI cookie is configured to follow you off ChatGPT
Jamie Larson at Buchodi's Threat Intel published a teardown on 20 September of how OpenAI's
ads pixel resolves off-site browsing to a ChatGPT identity. ChatGPT mints a 60-second RS256
JWT binding your account subject to a 22-character identifier, posts it to `bzr.openai.com`,
and gets back `__obi` — `Domain=.openai.com`, `Max-Age` one year, `SameSite=none; Secure`.
Every other OpenAI cookie tested was blocked on advertiser pages by `SameSite=Lax` or a
domain mismatch; `__obi` is the only one set up to travel. It is attached to the `<script src>`
that loads the pixel, so the disclosure happens before any OpenAI code runs and the SDK's
"no credentials" path does not help. The SDK also scrapes identity from the page — it replaces
`window.dataLayer.push` with its own function — and scraped identity outnumbered
advertiser-supplied identity 685 events to 255; email and phone are SHA-256 hashed, city and
postcode are not. Two details make this more than standard adtech: every sync token decoded
carried `consent_decision: analytics_allowed`, so refusing marketing consent does not turn it
off, and the anonymous variant is just as stable per device, persisting at least 27 days. The
author is careful about limits — Chrome on Android only, roughly one session in five produced
a token, and the server-side join is inferred from the design rather than observed. Questions
sent to OpenAI on 14 September got an acknowledgement and no answers.
[teardown](https://www.buchodi.com/chatgpt-now-knows-what-you-do-on-other-websites-via-ad-collector/)

## Homelab

### Jellyfin 12 is a one-way trip, so read the notes before `docker compose pull`
Jellyfin 12.0 arrived on 8 September and 12.1 followed on 15 September with 47 bugfixes,
which is a reasonable signal that the upgrade is now worth attempting. It is also the first
release in a long time you cannot back out of: the database changes mean rolling back
requires a full restore from backup, third-party plugins should be removed before migrating
and re-added afterwards, and a full library scan is **required** after the upgrade — auto-resolved
alternate versions get deleted during migration and only the rescan brings them back. Expect
that first scan to take much longer than usual and some titles to show up as newly added.
Direct upgrades from 10.10.7 and 10.11.x are supported; anything older should stop at 10.10.7
first. Debian Bullseye and Ubuntu Focal packages are no longer built. If you switched to the
unstable plugin repo at some point, switch it back.
[release notes](https://github.com/jellyfin/jellyfin/releases/tag/v12.0)

### llama.cpp's CUDA backend stopped rescoring the whole KV cache
A sparse flash-attention path for Qwen4 landed in llama.cpp on 20 September, continuing work
from an earlier PR. The problem it addresses is dull and expensive: the model's attention was
re-scoring the entire KV cache on every pass. The fix takes the union of tokens actually in
use across a group of eight columns, so the worst case is bounded, and switches to the sparse
kernel past twice that bound — 32,768 tokens of context for Qwen4. On a DGX Spark running an
A3B expert model at IQ1_S, prefill gains scale with depth: 1.08x at 10k tokens, 1.16x at 50k,
1.26x at 100k, with generation going 1.03x to 1.18x over the same range. If you keep a long
context open — a big repo, a long chat — this is where your tokens per second have been going.
[pull request](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/pull/28770)
