# Sat 17 Oct 2026

**The three that matter:** The kernel is removing the `Assisted-by:` tag it introduced for
AI-written patches, and Torvalds spent an email explaining why tool bans are stupid. The 28th
CGPM voted to make UTC continuous from 20 May 2027, ending leap seconds. And Claude traced
NIST's elliptic-curve seeds to `lrand48` seeded with 1997 timestamps.

## Dev + infra

### The kernel is deleting the tag it created for AI-assisted patches
Sasha Levin's 9 October patch strips `Assisted-by:` out of submitting-patches.rst,
coding-assistants.rst and checkpatch.pl, citing Maintainers Summit consensus to deprecate it.
Fifty-seven messages in, Torvalds backed removal with a reductio: then "don't use compilers for
any of your kernel submissions" either. Expectations of understanding stay; mandatory disclosure
of tooling does not.
[lore.kernel.org](https://lore.kernel.org/lkml/CAHk-=whTs09_ozN+e09GU68N1wPP56MUzDjrg9rnKxQUa0A_4Q@mail.gmail.com/)

### A signed 16-bit counter from the original BSD gives local root on OpenBSD
Calif.io overflowed the shared-memory attachment count — a `short`, 37 years old — by forking
repeatedly, raced it into a use-after-free, then chained an out-of-bounds kernel write. That is
root on 7.9 with KARL, SMAP and SMEP enabled and no information disclosure. Patched in errata
026 and 018; the allocators staying deterministic is what made it practical.
[Calif](https://calif.io/research/big-fish)

## AI research + releases

### NIST's curve seeds were lrand48 output, timestamped in 1997
Claude was asked cold where the ECDSA seeds came from and answered in under half an hour: a
scanner across 34 1990s PRNG combinations, a tell (four of ten seeds had a zero top bit in all
20 words), then LLL lattice reduction to recover `lrand48`'s 48-bit state. P-256's seed dates to
16 August 1997, 01:52:34, after 1,842 iterations. No backdoor, no weakness.
[Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/ecdsa-curve-origins)

### Mistral's forecaster is 209M parameters and wants no GPU
Timestral, out 15 October in 5M/28M/123M/209M sizes, does probabilistic multivariate
time-series forecasting across 21 quantiles with a 32,768-step context, under a modified MIT
licence. Mistral claims first place on GIFT-Eval, TIME and fev-bench, and 10–140ms inference on
ordinary server CPUs. The 28M beating larger models is the part worth testing.
[Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/timestral)

## Science + space

### Leap seconds end on 20 May 2027
Resolution 5 of the 28th CGPM makes UTC continuous from that date and caps |UT1−UTC| at 3,600
seconds, which holds for several centuries. The reason is the negative leap second nobody has
ever handled: a workshop put its probability at 30% by 2035, against infrastructure the
resolution calls largely unprepared. BIPM now coordinates the transition with ITU, IAU, IUGG
and IERS.
[BIPM](https://www.bipm.org/en/cgpm-2026/resolution-5)
