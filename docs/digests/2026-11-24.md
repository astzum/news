# Tue 24 Nov 2026

**The three that matter:** Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang eleven days
after he resigned, with input from the US government. Z.ai's GLM-5.4 reaches
2T-class results by running a shared block of 28 layers twice instead of adding
parameters. Ladybird's from-scratch browser engine is now a download, not a
build.

## Hardware

### Nvidia brought Jensen Huang back eleven days after he resigned
Huang left on 12 November after reporting he had been the target of a foreign
intelligence effort; CFO Colette Kress ran the company in the interim. The board
voted unanimously to reinstate him on 23 November, saying it received input from
senior levels of the US government and is confident there is no national
security risk. The independent-committee investigation continues.
[source](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-board-reinstates-jensen-huang-as-president-ceo-and-board-director)

### Ten years of EPYC is 12.1x the throughput and 5x the efficiency
Phoronix dusted off its 2017 dual EPYC 7601 Naples server and ran 100+
benchmarks against dual Zen 6 Venice parts on the same Ubuntu 26.10 stack.
Geometric mean: 12.1x for the 256-core EPYC 9996, 8.7x for the 96-core 9686F,
topping out at 133x in OpenVINO. Performance per watt improved only about 5x.
[source](https://www.phoronix.com/review/amd-epyc-9006-7601)

## AI research + releases

### GLM-5.4 buys depth by running 28 layers twice instead of adding parameters
Z.ai converted its 750B GLM-5 base into a Loop Transformer: a weight-shared
block runs two iterations, per-iteration gates write into the residual stream,
and the second pass reuses the first's sparse-attention indices and KV, so the
extra loop costs one sliding window rather than a full cache. Thinking output
drops 10–14%. Weights promised later; API "coming soon".
[source](https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.4)

### Gemini agents cut quantum circuit costs, with Lean checking the work
DeepMind set independent agent teams on known quantum algorithms, verified every
idea in Lean, and had the teams trade notes every four hours. An
inertial-confinement-fusion circuit got 11.8x cheaper, falling from 5,650 to
4,338 error-corrected qubits; the arcsin loading routine improved 290x. Still
beyond early hardware, but the Lean library and problem specs are open.
[source](https://deepmind.google/blog/finding-faster-quantum-algorithms-with-agents-and-formal-verification/)

## Dev + infra

### Ladybird's new engine is something you can download now
Andreas Kling shipped the first alpha for macOS and Linux — an engine written
from scratch, not a Chromium or Gecko fork. The rolling auto-update channel
matters more than the alpha label: until now you had to build from source to see
it improve. Crashes are expected, and it speaks WebDriver and the Firefox
DevTools protocol.
[source](https://ladybird.org/posts/alpha/)

### macOS userspace now runs as a Linux kernel module
xnulinux builds XNU from Apple's published sources and loads it into a stock
arm64 Linux kernel as `xnu.ko`. There is no hypervisor: hosted macOS processes
run on pinned Linux kernel threads, with a GPL interface module lending memory,
timers, files, network and GPU. macOS 27.2.1 boots to Finder and Safari in
31–48s on Apple Silicon.
[source](https://github.com/AFK-surf/xnulinux)

## Homelab

### A 27B model at BF16 quality in 10 GB, if you run their fork
Nari Labs' trellis-coded quantisation of Qwen3.8-27B matches BF16 on DeepSWE and
Terminal-Bench at 3 bits (10.1 GB) and 2.5 bits (8.4 GB); the 2-bit file falls
over on maths. The catch is real — it needs their llama.cpp fork, stock
llama.cpp, Ollama and LM Studio can't load the tensor types, and Ampere, Ada and
Apple GPUs are slower than ordinary GGUFs.
[source](https://huggingface.co/nari-labs/Qwen3.8-27B-NariQ-GGUF)

## Science + space

### NASA is taking bids on a 20 kW fission reactor for the Moon
Lunar Reactor-1 is out for proposals, due 21 December, with a launch-ready
system targeted for 2030 and at least five years of surface operation. The case
is the two-week lunar night, which solar cannot cover. It must meet DOE nuclear
safety standards, and sits alongside SR-1 Freedom, a 2028 nuclear-electric
propulsion demo bound for Mars.
[source](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/rtmd/nasa-seeks-partners-to-design-first-nuclear-reactor-for-the-moon/)
