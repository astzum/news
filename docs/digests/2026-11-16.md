# Mon 16 Nov 2026

**The three that matter:** SpaceX caught a Starship upper stage for the first
time, about 22 hours after launch. ULA's Vulcan failed ten minutes into its
first Amazon Leo flight and took 40 satellites with it. Two newly installed
routers flooded the FAA's decades-old copper network and grounded every US
departure for four hours.

## Science + space

### SpaceX caught a Starship upper stage for the first time
Ship 42 came back from orbit on Saturday evening, relit three Raptors to flip
vertical, and settled into the tower arms roughly 22.5 hours after Friday's
launch. The booster didn't play along — it diverted to a Gulf splashdown. Two
nubs protruding about a foot each are all that hold a returning ship on those
arms.
[source](https://arstechnica.com/space/2026/11/with-starship-catch-spacex-still-shows-it-can-dazzle-the-world/)

### Vulcan's first total mission failure cost Amazon 40 satellites
It lifted off from Space Launch Complex 41 at 12:40pm ET Sunday and, per ULA's
own statement, "suffered an anomaly 10 minutes into the flight." This was
Vulcan's fifth launch and its first in nine months, after solid-booster nozzle
burn-throughs paused Space Force missions. The boosters behaved this time; ULA
says it has no data yet on what didn't.
[source](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/launches-spacecraft/ula-vulcan-centaur-return-to-flight-amazon-leo-internet-satellite-launch)

## Dev + infra

### A thousand connections every four seconds grounded every US flight
FAA technology chief Rebecca Guy says two newly installed Juniper routers — at a
radio site in El Dorado, Arkansas and a radar site in Amarillo, Texas — pinged
the agency's network at about a thousand connections every four to five seconds.
The decades-old copper backbone folded. Staff disabled the offending feature
network-wide; HPE says the routers "did not fail."
[source](https://www.tomshardware.com/networking/two-hpe-juniper-network-routers-grounded-all-the-us-flights-on-friday-faas-decades-old-copper-network-buckled-under-a-feature-hpe-says-worked-as-intended-creaking-air-traffic-communication-system-became-overloaded-by-very-high-rate-pinging)

### Thousands of Lovable apps hand their database to anyone who asks
Crissy Field pulled 159,041 Lovable projects out of Common Crawl; 34,545 used
Supabase and 24,664 returned database rows on page load with no login at all.
Read-only follow-up requests pulled records containing email addresses from
7,403 of those. Among the finds: 80,000 forum profiles, 200,000 chat messages,
and over 1,000 passport numbers with expiry dates.
[source](https://www.crissyfield.de/research/lovable-en/)

### PyPI will now tell you where a release file actually came from
The redesigned project pages add a security tab with per-file attestations and a
computed provenance state per release — full, none, or one of several warnings
including inconsistent provenance and loss of provenance. It implements the
OpenSSF Attestations Style Guide. Handy if you have been pinning dependencies on
vibes.
[source](https://blog.pypi.org/posts/2026-11-06-ui-updates-complete/)

## AI research + releases

### IBM put the context structure in reserved tokens and injection mostly stopped
LLMON is XML-ish markup whose delimiters are special tokens rather than
characters, so structure and content occupy separate parts of the vocabulary,
and unique IDs bind each instruction to the exact data it may act on. IBM
reports near-zero attack success on some injection benchmarks. Two Granite 4.1
previews, 3B and 8B, plus lossless JSON converters.
[source](https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-granite/llmon-an-llm-native-markup-language-now-in-granite)

## Hardware

### AMD put 256 Zen 6 cores in an embedded socket with a seven-year shelf life
EPYC Embedded 9006 on SP7, with PCIe Gen 6 and CXL. AMD's own numbers — internal
estimates, not measurements — are 1.8x throughput, 2.7x memory bandwidth and
1.5x I/O bandwidth over the last generation. The named targets are semiconductor
test rigs, firewalls and storage controllers, where the long availability
guarantee matters more than the core count.
[source](https://www.amd.com/en/products/embedded/epyc/9006-series.html)
