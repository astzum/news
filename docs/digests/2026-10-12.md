# Mon 12 Oct 2026

**The three that matter:** A drone strike destroyed Yandex's Vladimir data centre, and Yandex
Cloud now tells customers to rebuild on other providers. CrowdStrike traces a wave of South
Korean bank intrusions to what looks like one person driving an open-source agentic pentest
tool across four models. And CPU, DRAM and SSD are now 68% of the build cost of a mid-range
laptop.

## Dev + infra

### Yandex Cloud is telling its customers to move to other providers
Incident 2136 opened 01:00 UTC on 11 October: a UAV attack damaged Yandex's Vladimir data
centre and operations there are fully suspended, with no casualties. Yandex calls the
remaining configuration unstable, describes the platform as in emergency mode, and has
suspended quota increases. The ru-central1-b zone has been down since a 7 October power
failure. Compute is partial; Monium is out.
[status](https://status.yandex.cloud/en/incidents/2136)

### drgn 0.3.0 hardens a kernel debugger against the dumps you feed it
Out 10 October, and a useful reminder that if you analyse other people's core dumps your
debugger is attack surface. Fixed: buffer overflows, out-of-bounds access, use-after-free,
double-free and integer overflow in handling of debug info and core dumps — some reachable
with crafted input — plus many missing error checks. Also Linux 7.2/7.3 support and direct
memory reads from QEMU guests.
[release](https://github.com/osandov/drgn/releases/tag/v0.3.0)

## AI industry

### CrowdStrike pins the South Korean bank intrusions on one operator and four models
Its 7 October report has them running ARTEX, a Chinese open-source agentic pentest tool, with
DeepSeek v4.1-flash as the backend plus GLM-5.3 and Grok 4.6, and Claude Code alongside for
recon and vulnerability research. Two banks are confirmed compromised; the full count isn't.
Attribution is moderate confidence and rests on Chinese-language prompts and tool choice, not
nationality.
[report](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/unknown-threat-actor-uses-artex-to-target-south-korean-finance/)

## Hardware

### CPU, DRAM and SSD are 68% of the BOM on a US$900 laptop
TrendForce's 8 October note puts that share at 3Q26 and projects global notebook shipments
down a low single-digit percentage in 2027 — high single digits if brands pass the full
component cost through. The alternatives are thinner margins or cutting memory capacity. This
is the supply-side half of the DDR4-revival story everyone is reading.
[research note](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20261008-13264.html)

## Homelab

### A Pi-hole-style DNS blocker that runs on a bare ESP32-C3
No PSRAM needed: domains live in flash as 40-bit FNV-1a hashes, so about 141k fit in 0.67MB,
or 537k with a single-app partition, using roughly 50KB of RAM against 2.5MB for the obvious
string approach. About 10ms per lookup including WiFi, via ~18 binary-search flash reads. Web
dashboard, OTA blocklist updates, captive-portal setup. MIT.
[repo](https://github.com/M-Abozaid/esp32-c3-adblock)

## AI research + releases

### Byte-level transformers beat subword ones as you scale, given the right training
Wang and co-authors add token-superposition training and hash embeddings, and byte models pull
ahead of subword Transformers as size increases. The models invent their own segmentation:
certain positions collect local context, and up to 25% of intermediate layers can be
restricted to those local representations at no cost. Speculative decoding accepts 3.4× more
tokens. Preprint, 5 October.
[paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05978)

## Science + space

### A planet may have condensed out of its own star's ashes
Hubble spectra of white dwarf HS 0209+0832 sat in the archive from 1999 with ~100
unidentified features; a Warwick doctoral student rechecked them against a newer database and
found niobium, an element forged only in a dying star. FUSE confirmed it, TESS saw a planet at
about 6 million km over four months. Candidate only. Nature Astronomy, 5 October.
[ESA/Hubble](https://esahubble.org/news/heic2613/) · [paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-026-02983-7)
