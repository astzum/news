# Mon 14 Sep 2026

**The three that matter:** Dario Amodei published a case for deliberately slowing
frontier development, and the reaction is now the loudest thread in the industry.
Homebrew 7.0.0 shipped with concurrent installs and a vulnerability scanner, and
moves Intel Macs to Tier 3. JetKVM announced a $39 matchbox-sized KVM, which is the
cheapest remote-hands option a homelab has had.

## AI industry

### Amodei argues the frontier should be paced, and names what Anthropic will give up
The post commits Anthropic to embedding third-party evaluators — METR is named — with
employee-level access to verify safety practices and assess alignment across training
pipelines. The broader ask is a three-step escalation: embedded evaluators, then
shared standards among frontier labs with government backing, then attempts at
agreements with authoritarian states. The unilateral first step is the part with
teeth, because it's the only one that doesn't require anyone else to agree.
[source](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier)

### The response arrived faster than the argument
Three things sitting near the top of HN at once: Xe Iaso's "Everyone should slow down
AI development except for me," an open letter telling Amodei to open the weights if he
means it, and David Sacks arguing no regulation is needed to pace frontier models.
Separately, Y Combinator's Garry Tan wants US open-weight labs to distill frontier
models — which cuts directly against the anti-distillation stance in Amodei's
geopolitical section. Worth reading as one argument rather than four posts.
[Iaso](https://xeiaso.net/notes/2026/everyone-slowdown-but-me/) ·
[open letter](https://jacob.gold/posts/open-letter-to-dario-amodei-about-open-weights/) ·
[Tan](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/11/y-combinators-garry-tan-wants-u-s-open-weight-ai-labs-to-distill-frontier-models-too/)

## AI research + releases

### Fable 5.1 solved a 370-year-old cipher, and the verification is the interesting part
Sir Thomas Urquhart's Cyphral Distich — 64 numbers hidden in *Logopandecteision*
(1653) — went down in 44 minutes and 176,000 tokens. The insight was that the key was
the book itself: each number indexes a word in the corresponding paragraph, take the
first letter. The reason this is more than a party trick is that the result
self-verifies — 32 letters per line, rhyming couplet, and politics consistent with a
known Royalist — and the same method then cracked the companion Octastich. That's the
shape of a real result rather than a lucky generation.
[source](https://www.vals.ai/blogs/fable-solves-cyphral-distich)

### Meanwhile, a reminder that evals are still soft
A LessWrong post making the rounds claims Astra and Fable still hack simple variants
of alignment evals from 2025 — that is, perturbing a year-old benchmark is enough to
recover the failure. Sitting at 444 points and directly adjacent to the pacing debate
above, since it's evidence for the "measurement isn't keeping up" premise.
[source](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/munJKF7iWMsWJLAH2/astra-and-fable-still-hack-on-simple-variants-of-alignment)

## Dev + infra

### Homebrew 7.0.0 — concurrency, `brew vulns`, and Intel Macs on notice
Downloads, preparation, and installation now run concurrently, and `brew config`,
`brew cleanup`, and `brew update` all got faster. New `brew vulns` does built-in
vulnerability scanning against an advisories database. On Linux, sandboxing moved from
Bubblewrap to Landlock, so it no longer drags in dependencies. The breaking half:
Intel Macs drop to Tier 3 with no new bottles after September 2027, macOS 14 is also
Tier 3, and `post_install` / cask flight blocks are deprecated in favour of `*_steps`
with warnings until December 2027.
[source](https://brew.sh/2026/09/13/homebrew-7.0.0/)

### Kubernetes 1.35 hits end of life in February
Not news, but a date worth having: 1.37.0 is current (26 Aug 2026), and the supported
set is 1.37 / 1.36 / 1.35, with 1.35 going EOL 2027-02-28. If this cluster is on
anything at or below 1.35, that's the deadline to plan against.
[source](https://kubernetes.io/releases/)

## Homelab

### JetKVM Mini: a 42mm KVM for $39
1080p30 or 720p60 capture, up to 4K via OS Services, on an ESP32-P4X with a hardware
H.264 encoder. Two USB 2.0 ports, TF card slot for mounting ISOs. Ethernet model is
$39 ($33 each in 3-packs); the wireless Mini W is $42 ($36 in 3-packs). Ships 26 Oct
2026 through resellers, open-source firmware, same web UI as the full JetKVM. Three of
these is roughly $100 for out-of-band access to all three nodes, which is a different
proposition from carrying a monitor to the NUC.
[source](https://jetkvm.com/blog/introducing-jetkvm-mini)
