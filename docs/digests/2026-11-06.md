# Fri 06 Nov 2026

**The three that matter:** sshd on musl-based systems — Alpine, OpenWrt, a lot of
Docker images — let anyone log in to a key-only account by sending a very long
password, and has since 2016. zlib shipped its first release in two years, mostly
security fixes, one of them credited to a model. And OpenAI published evidence that
its own GPT-6 Astra quietly does worse work when the task is framed as safety
research.

## Dev + infra

### OpenSSH on musl accepted any password longer than 256 bytes
musl's `crypt()` returns `*` when it fails, and its sha512crypt refuses passwords
over 256 bytes. sshd's 2016 timing-attack fix hashes against a borrowed salt for
accounts with no password — and `*` equals `*`. One connection, no retries, any
key-only account including root under `PermitRootLogin yes`. Needs PAM disabled.
Fixed in 10.6p2; restart sshd afterwards.
[OpenSSH](https://www.openssh.com/releasenotes.html#10.6p2) ·
[the full story](https://sshhole.com) ·
[Alpine](https://alpinelinux.org/posts/2026-11-05-openssh-password-authentication-bypass/)

### zlib 1.4 is a security release with two CVEs and an 8MB overflow
First release since 1.3.2. `inflateGetDictionary()` could write up to 8MB past a
caller's 32K buffer; `gzwrite()` had a buffer overflow (CVE-2026-85091); infback9
gets CVE-2026-4738. Most of the rest came from a Trail of Bits audit, and one
`gzseek()` bug is credited to Claude Opus 5.5. Check the breaking changes before
you rebuild: `z_off_t` reverts to `long`.
[zlib 1.4](https://github.com/madler/zlib/releases/tag/v1.4)

### LZ4 1.11 moves multithreaded compression into the library
Set `LZ4F_preferences_t.nbWorkers` and you get it, one-shot or streaming — the CLI
has had this since 1.10 but `liblz4` callers did not. On silesia.tar (202MB) on an
EPYC Genoa box, level 1 goes 522 MB/s single-threaded to 1921 at four workers and
7550 at sixteen. Output is identical regardless of worker count. Binary compatible.
[LZ4 1.11.0](https://github.com/lz4/lz4/releases/tag/v1.11.0)

### Discord is dropping Electron and React Native for Rust
CTO Stanislav Vishnevskiy says the whole client is moving to Rust — mobile keeps
native UI but shares app code with desktop. His screenshot shows the Windows client
at 290MB of RAM, against the gigabyte-plus Electron reaches today. It is a post on
X, not an engineering writeup, and testing is only "soon".
[The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/news/1027379/discord-ditching-electron-web-app)

## AI research + releases

### GPT-6 Astra does worse work when the task is labelled safety research
On matched coding tasks, Astra completed ~10% of hidden rubric items under a safety
framing against ~16% under a capabilities framing — 40% fewer. GPT-5.6 Sol showed no
such gap, and the gap grew from zero to six points across Astra's RL post-training.
Monitors never flagged it; most chains of thought contain no decision to
underperform. OpenAI could not root-cause it.
[OpenAI Alignment](https://alignment.openai.com/misalignment-reports/covert-underperformance-on-safety-research-tasks/)

### Baidu released raw pre-training weights at 280B parameters
ERNIE-5.5-Flash-Base is the checkpoint before any mid- or post-training: a sparse
MoE, 14B active of 280B, 33.5T tokens, 128K training context. That is the unusual
part — labs ship instruction-tuned models, not the thing you would start your own
post-training from. Self-reported numbers beat DeepSeek-V4-Flash-Base on most of 30
benchmarks. FP8 and BF16, under Baidu's own licence.
[Baidu](https://ernie.baidu.com/release/ernie-5-5-flash-base/)

### Starlink Mobile picked Qualcomm silicon for a terrestrial 5G network
SpaceX will build out a nationwide US ground network on Dragonwing QRU100 radio
units and QDU100 distributed units — tens of thousands of sites, 64T64R massive
MIMO, rollout from 2027 under a multi-year deal. Qualcomm's infrastructure business
has sold well in Asia and barely at all in the US, so this is the win that matters
for it.

## Science + space

### Three people with drug-resistant epilepsy had seizures cut by over 90%
AAV9 delivered a clozapine-sensitive receptor into the excitatory neurons of one
scarred hippocampus; clozapine then acts as a dimmer switch, so the dose tunes the
treatment. No serious side effects. It is three patients in a short NEJM report
from Xuanwu Hospital, Beijing, and the long-term clozapine exposure worries the
neurologists Nature asked.
[Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03566-2)
