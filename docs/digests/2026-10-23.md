# Fri 23 Oct 2026

**The three that matter:** David Harvey, who proved the O(n log n) bound for integer
multiplication, has read an AI-written manuscript claiming to beat it and thinks the
claim is plausible. Railway says an intruder decrypted some customer environment
variables, so every key stored there needs rotating. And Modular published the rest of
the MAX source under a licence that is explicitly not open source.

## AI research + releases

### The o(n log n) integer multiplication claim out of an AI maths dump looks plausible
Harvey — who with van der Hoeven proved the O(n log n) bound in the 2021 *Annals* —
spent 38 pages on one manuscript an unnamed US company dumped on GitHub. It claims
O(n(log n)^(1−ε)), long believed impossible. His verdict: the bound is "plausible",
though he has "not checked most of the details". Turing-model data rearrangement costs
remain unexamined.
[David Harvey (PDF)](https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~davidharvey/research/subnlogn.pdf)

### A 360M model trained for 75 GPU-hours imitates a terminal well enough to run vim
Qwen2.5-Coder-0.5B, fine-tuned on 336k recorded Docker sessions (~2B tokens) with its
vocabulary pruned to 333 tokens, predicts screen state from keypresses — no shell, no
filesystem underneath. 208MB at int4, ~60ms per character on an M4, 80/106 on
ShellBench, and files it invents persist across a session. Weights promised; code not
released.
[simedw](https://simedw.com/2026/10/21/world-model-terminal/)

## Dev + infra

### Railway says attackers decrypted some customer environment variables
Detected the morning of 21 October; deployments were paused during the investigation.
Encrypted env vars were accessed and the decrypted contents of some obtained, putting
API keys plus database and registry credentials in scope. Railway calls it part of a
campaign against platform companies aimed at compromising AI inference providers, and
has asked upstream providers to revoke keys. Rotate everything.
[Railway](https://blog.railway.com/p/security-disclosure-wednesday-october-21st)

### Quad9 is leaving France rather than block domains for the whole world
A French court sided with beIN over sports piracy and attached a €1,000-a-day penalty
for non-compliance. Quad9 argues recursive DNS gives it no reliable way to map users to
jurisdictions, leaving only global blocking or the geolocation filtering that already
failed it in Germany. Service ends 24 November, and roughly 20 similar suits are still
pending.
[Quad9](https://quad9.net/news/blog/au-revoir-france/)

## AI industry

### Modular's MAX source is now public, and the licence is not open source
The graph compiler, runtime and CPU/GPU backends ship with 26.7 under the new MAX
License: unlimited free use on any hardware, commercial products included. You may not
publish modified source except as a fork contributing back, sell hosted inference
without displaying a "Modular Accelerated" wordmark, or feed MAX to a model to
reimplement it. Kernels and Python APIs stay Apache 2.0.
[Modular](https://www.modular.com/blog/max-license)

## Hardware

### The Switch 2's first jailbreak lands through the filesystem sysmodule
ns2jb enters via WebKit, then triggers a 0x301-byte heap overflow in the `fs`
sysmodule's path normalisation using backslash parent-directory components — arbitrary
read/write, code execution as `fs` itself, and MMIO access to hardware. It runs on
23.0.1, the current firmware, but it is tethered and obviously patchable. Demonstrated
by modding Mario Kart World.
[pepsipu](https://pepsipu.com/blog/2026-10-switch2-jailbreak-ns2jb/)

## Science + space

### NASA's first fission-powered deep space probe has a power-conversion contractor
Sierra Lobo will build the closed Brayton cycle system turning reactor heat into
electricity for SR-1 Freedom, with Idaho National Laboratory designing the reactor
itself, which stays inactive until the spacecraft leaves Earth orbit. Launch is late
2028 for Mars orbit, carrying SkyFall: three Ingenuity-derived helicopters. The same
tech is meant to power lunar night operations.
[NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/glenn/sr-1-freedom-advances-with-progress-on-nuclear-power-module/)
