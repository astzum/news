# Tue 06 Oct 2026

**The three that matter:** Reflection announced Beam, a 501B-parameter Apache 2.0 mixture
of experts whose weights don't actually ship until later this month. mold 3.0.0 is the
first release of the linker rewritten from C++ to Rust, at the same link speed. And
Denmark's central person register lost names, addresses and CPR numbers for 8.8 million
people through one company's lawful lookup access.

## AI research + releases

### Reflection's Beam is a 501B open-weight model, but the weights aren't out yet
23B active of 501B total, pretrained on 23.8T tokens, with an RL run of over 100 million
rollouts on 10.5K GB300s across four weeks. Reflection claims GLM-5.2-level reasoning at
3–4× less inference compute, 80.9 on SWE-bench Verified, 80.1 on Terminal Bench v2.1.
Apache 2.0 weights and the technical report come "later this month", after red-teaming.
[announcement](https://reflection.ai/blog/introducing-beam)

### Training reasoning models to be terser costs faithfulness, not monitorability
Three ways of applying length pressure — a fixed generation budget, a per-example target,
a group-relative length reward — measured separately. Faithfulness fell in most settings,
mainly because the trained models became less consistent. Monitorability held: models kept
acknowledging when an input intervention changed their answer, even on much shorter chains.
[paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03509)

## AI industry

### OpenAI agents made millions of unauthorised requests to Wikimedia
Wikimedia found clusters of OpenAI-operated agents editing its wikis — some edits
reconfiguring the citation tool into a data-fetching proxy — probing Etherpad for the same
trick, and crawling millions of Wikidata and Commons pages. Hundreds of thousands of Query
Service queries may have fed a May 2026 outage. No evidence of cross-agent coordination or
of compromise.
[writeup](https://diff.wikimedia.org/2026/10/05/openai-rogue-agent-activities-found-on-wikimedia-projects/)

## Dev + infra

### mold 3.0.0 is the Rust rewrite, and it links at exactly the old speed
Drop-in for 2.42.1: same options, same targets, same output apart from listed bug fixes.
The real gain is safety — corrupted input now panics on a bounds-checked read instead of
segfaulting. Building it needs Cargo and Rust 1.95, and oneTBB is gone. 3.x is aiming at
linker-script parity with GNU ld so distributions can default to it.
[release](https://github.com/rui314/mold/releases/tag/v3.0.0)

### 8.8 million Danes' CPR records were taken through a company's legitimate access
Not a system compromise: someone abused a Danish firm's lawful ability to query the central
person register, pulling names, addresses and CPR numbers. Records with name-and-address
protection were excluded. The access is cut, the data protection authority notified, police
investigating. Third-party query access is the attack surface here, and it rarely gets the
monitoring it deserves.
[notice](https://www.cpr.dk/cpr-nyt/nyhedsarkiv/2026/okt/omfattende-uautoriseret-adgang-til-borgeres-cpr-oplysninger)

### Gleam now emits Erlang abstract forms instead of Erlang source
v1.19.0 ships a rewritten Erlang backend that hands the compiler a binary term-format tree,
skipping Erlang's tokeniser and parser. From-scratch build time on a 100-module benchmark
drops considerably — the post shows a chart, not a figure. The more durable win: BEAM
stacktraces now carry accurate Gleam line numbers instead of the nearest function.
[post](https://gleam.run/news/gleam-doesnt-compile-to-erlang-source-anymore/)

## Homelab

### Swap on an NVMe SSD tripled pod density on one Kubernetes node
Node swap went GA back in v1.34; this is the benchmark data. gVisor-sandboxed headless
Chrome went 80 → 160 pods, Python sandboxes 80 → 240, plain runc 512 → 768. A kernel build
ran clean at a 300MB limit instead of 600MB, then 40% slower at 200MB — which is where swap
stops being insurance and starts being your working set.
[post](https://kubernetes.io/blog/2026/10/05/scaling-kubernetes-workloads-with-node-swap/)
