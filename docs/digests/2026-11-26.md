# Thu 26 Nov 2026

**The three that matter:** OpenAI pushed nine more machine-written, Lean-checked
results overnight, including Smale's ninth problem and an exact matrix
multiplication exponent. Google's Cogentic proved unconditional bounds for
integer factoring and small-characteristic discrete logs, which is a real result
and not a threat to RSA. Richard Lipton, co-author of the 1979 paper this whole
enterprise argues with, says the flood is pollution rather than progress.

## AI research + releases

### OpenAI's nine overnight manuscripts include Smale's ninth problem
Nine papers in two families, with Lean formalisations of selected results. A
deterministic strongly polynomial algorithm for linear programming answers
Smale's ninth; the square matrix multiplication exponent is pinned at exactly
9/4 over every field; the polynomial Hirsch conjecture is disproved; the
sunflower conjecture proved. Two of them subsume yesterday's VP ≠ VNP paper,
which was 24 hours old.
[source](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/history.md)

### Google's unconditional factoring result does not touch RSA
Gemini 4 inside an agent loop, one prompt, no human in the chain. The factoring
algorithm runs in expected L_N[1/3, 105] — the number field sieve's exponent,
proven rather than heuristic, but with a constant of 105 it is far slower than
what anyone actually runs. The discrete-log result is the sharper one:
sub-quasi-polynomial in fixed small characteristic.
[source](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/master/cogentic_research_papers)

### Lipton, 47 years on: the proof flood is pollution
DeMillo and Lipton revisit their 1979 argument that proofs are a social process
of critique and internalisation, against OpenAI's October dump of 722
manuscripts claiming 372 open problems. The objection is structural rather than
sour: posting is not submitting, and human attention is the fixed resource that
loses the race. Read it before the next batch lands.
[source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31286)

## Dev + infra

### Hex.pm ran attacker commands for 13 minutes and logged everyone out
69 malicious requests between 18:16 and 18:29 UTC on 23 November gave command
execution on six web servers; a firewall rule landed at 18:27 and patched builds
by 18:31. No package tampering was found, but database reads are not logged, so
password hashes, API key hashes and 2FA secrets can't be cleared either way.
Rotate both, and avoid mirrors.
[source](https://hex.pm/blog/security-incident-on-hexpm)

### 1Password's breach came in through an employee's GitHub account
The attacker read source and pushed malicious code to backend services, which
then downloaded customer names, emails, account identifiers, SRP parameters and —
the part that matters — tokens embedded in invitation, provisioning and recovery
emails. Vault contents were never reachable. Treat any unexpected 1Password
invite or recovery link as hostile for a while.
[source](https://1password.com/blog/update-on-nov-2026-security-incident)

## Hardware

### A fanless Panther Lake box idles at 1.5W and sustains 25W
ServeTheHome measured Supermicro's SYS-E103-14P-H: the Core Ultra X7 368H holds
53–55W for roughly 20 seconds, then settles at 22–25W, with package idle of
1.4–1.8W. Single-core scored 843 against 738 for a fanned ASRock 358H.
Core-to-core latency shows the three core types plainly — 25–30ns inside the
P-cores, 109–113ns out to the low-power E-cores.
[source](https://www.servethehome.com/supermicro-sys-e103-14p-h-fanless-panther-lake-review/)

## Science + space

### NASA has frozen the design of its first fission-powered deep-space craft
SR-1 Freedom cleared its mission design review in October and is targeted at
late 2028, carrying the SkyFall capsule and three helicopters to Mars under
nuclear electric propulsion. The reactor boom got shorter and now sits closer to
the spacecraft body on the strength of better shielding. First demonstration of
fission power beyond Earth orbit.
[source](https://www.nasa.gov/general/nasa-locks-in-sr1-freedom-mission-architecture/)
