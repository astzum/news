# Thu 01 Oct 2026

**The three that matter:** Google's new frontier model goes to cyber defenders before
anyone else, and to that group with the cyber guardrails deliberately switched off. OpenAI
says it caught operators replaying encrypted reasoning out of its models and attributes the
core of it to people associated with Moonshot AI. And the EDG C++ front end has stopped
being a licensed product and become public code under the C++ Alliance.

## AI research + releases

### Gemini 4 Argon ships to cyber defenders first, guardrails off
Announced 30 September and rolling out through the Fairwind Program before developers or
consumers get it; for trusted defenders and Google's own teams it ships without cyber
guardrails. Output limit goes from 64K to 1M tokens. DeepSWE v1.1 77.9%, CWE-bench v1 68%
(tied first). Introductory pricing $2 and $10 per million, cached input 95% off.
[announcement](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/)

### OpenAI says people tied to Moonshot AI farmed its models' hidden reasoning
Nothing was broken into: operators copied encrypted reasoning out of one conversation and
asked a model in another to decrypt and transcribe it. 16,000 such requests from over 4,000
users spiked on 24–25 July; a cluster of 15,000+ users was disrupted by 28 July. Shared
through the Frontier Model Forum — assume replayable reasoning artifacts are a shared risk.
[report](https://openai.com/index/disrupting-a-coordinated-model-distillation-campaign)

## Dev + infra

### The EDG C++ front end is now public code with a nonprofit home
As of 30 September it moves from proprietary licensing to github.com/edgcpp, with the C++
Alliance as fiscal sponsor and John Spicer still leading development. Annual licence fees
are replaced by community donations, and collectively funded features release to everyone
simultaneously — "one codebase, for everyone", no early-access tier for the payers.
[transition](https://edgcpp.org/#transition)

### Netlify moved edge functions off V8 isolates onto Firecracker microVMs
Median warm invocation dropped from 25–40ms to roughly 5–6ms, P99 47.4% faster, cold start
about 9ms on 1.2% of invocations, 99.998% availability. Functions now execute on Netlify's
own edge nodes instead of leaving the network, with rendezvous hashing for cache locality.
No user code changes. Read it for the snapshot/restore and scale-to-zero design.
[post](https://www.netlify.com/blog/edge-functions-firecracker-microvms/)

### The Dutch government's Windows replacement is a Nix flake
DAWO — Digitaal Autonome Werkomgeving Overheid — builds identical government workstations
from one flake covering hardware, bootloader, hardening, networking, GUI, users and
services. The code moved off code.overheid.nl to Codeberg so outsiders can contribute: 132
commits, four releases, Nix throughout. The goal is a digitally autonomous, interoperable
base for public-sector desktops.
[repository](https://codeberg.org/DAWO/DAWO-Core)

## Homelab

### Gitea 28.0.0 changes your egress rules out from under you
Released 30 September. Git operations now route through an internal proxy and the
`external` egress preset is gone, so review allow and block lists before upgrading. Git
2.25 or newer is required or it refuses to start, self-registration is off by default, and
`[server] DOMAIN` is ignored in favour of `ROOT_URL`. Completed Actions runs delete after
400 days.
[release notes](https://blog.gitea.com/release-of-28.0.0/)

### Backblaze's quarterly drive failure rate is the highest in a while
1.73% across 354,415 drives in Q2 2026, with lifetime AFR ticking up to 1.41%. The post
puts the jump down to ageing drives and small sample sizes rather than anything systemic.
Also worth knowing before you read the tables: there are still no SMR drives anywhere in
the fleet.
[drive stats](https://www.backblaze.com/blog/backblaze-drive-stats-for-q2-2026/)

### Three Jev-compatible decision models, trained at home on one GPU
Jeff returns calibrated probabilities over a list of options in a single forward pass, no
text generation to parse. 22ms median on an RTX PRO 6000, 28ms on an M4 Max; 79.1% at 0.8B
and 82.0% at 2B across five benchmarks against Jev's published 83.0%. Two to 3.5 hours to
train. MIT code, Apache-2.0 weights.
[repository](https://github.com/firelex/jeff)
