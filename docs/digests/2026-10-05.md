# Mon 05 Oct 2026

**The three that matter:** Google has stopped accepting product vulnerability reports to
its open source bug bounty, blaming a flood of AI-generated submissions. A new engine runs
a 125B model on a 12GB gaming GPU at 94 tokens/s. And a consultancy owner documented a
targeted attack that hid its payload in a git `post-checkout` hook.

## AI industry

### AI slop has closed Google's open source bug bounty to product vulnerabilities
As of 1 October the OSS VRP no longer takes product vulnerability reports. The rules blame
hallucinated reports plus findings that are technically real but unreachable or irrelevant
to the project's threat model. Supply chain compromises stay in scope; Cloud-adjacent repos
route to the Cloud VRP. An update is promised in Q1 2027.
[rules](https://bughunters.google.com/about/rules/open-source/google-open-source-software-vulnerability-reward-program-rules)

### OpenAI has put a shutdown date on gpt-5.1 and gpt-5.3-codex
Announced 1 October, both leave the API on 1 April 2027 with gpt-6-sol as the replacement;
gpt-5.4-nano goes the same day to gpt-6-luna. That is six months of notice. The older
text-to-speech models — `tts-1`, `tts-1-hd` and the GPT-4o mini TTS variants — go sooner,
on 6 January 2027, migrating to `gpt-realtime-2.1-mini`.
[deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)

## AI research + releases

### Teaching a coding agent when to compact its own context is worth 9.2 points
AutoCompact folds the compaction decision into the policy rather than the harness. A judge
corrects the base agent's compaction calls, each trajectory continues from the corrected
decision, then SFT plus RL on task success. Gains are absolute: 9.2% on SWE-bench Verified,
5.0% on SWE-PolyBench Verified, holding at every inference budget tested.
[paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02163)

### No open-weight model clears 42% at writing correct Kali commands
KaliBench is 8,504 query–command pairs over 1,642 tools, scored on exact match with
alias-aware canonicalisation. Across 24 configurations nothing beat 42% without tool hints
— flag bindings and argument order are where they break. Fine-tuning an 8B model on the
benchmark's verifiable rewards matched a 685B MoE.
[paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02206)

## Dev + infra

### A fake client sent a Dropbox repo with a credential stealer in a git hook
The pitch was an Ed Tech project with the NDA "in a separate branch". Checking out that
branch fired a `post-checkout` hook that downloaded an OS-specific binary, ran it and
deleted itself, with a Vercel app as C2. Frank Wiles found it by reading `.git/hooks` —
which is now the thing to do before checking out a stranger's repo.
[writeup](https://frankwiles.com/posts/i-got-targeted/)

### Cloudflare Artifacts hits open beta, and billing starts 15 October
Git repositories as a Workers primitive, pitched at one repo per agent or session: Workers
Builds deploys from them, an Artifacts binding manages them programmatically, event
subscriptions fire on push and clone, and data can be pinned to the US or EU. Workers Paid
only. The attached competition closes 14 October, $25k in credits for first.
[post](https://blog.cloudflare.com/next-git-platform-on-cloudflare/)

### Strata runs a 125B model on a 12GB gaming card
Qwen3.8-Flash-Next at Q2_0 on an RTX 5070 with 64GB RAM: 94 tokens/s out, 2,650 tokens/s
prefill on a 32K prompt. An RX 9070 XT does 60 and 1,160. Floor is 12GB VRAM, 32GB RAM and
a 70GB download; MIT, with an OpenAI-compatible server. Hacker News rounded the claim up to
an RTX 4090 at 100 t/s — the README's tables are the real numbers.
[project](https://github.com/Niko1221/Strata)
