# Tue 20 Oct 2026

**The three that matter:** Linux 7.3 shipped on schedule and is the biggest release
ever by every measure. Optical fibre has become the AI buildout's first real physical
bottleneck, with spot prices at 2.8–5x last year's. And MiniMax put out a Flash model
at $0.15/$0.60 per million tokens that had already been load-testing itself in public
under a codename.

## Dev + infra

### Linux 7.3 is the biggest kernel release on record
Out on 18 October: 18,765 non-merge commits from 3,021 developers, 807 of them
first-timers — records on all three counts. Notable inside: `fchroot()`, global per-CPU
data for BPF programs, working-set tracking for VMs, the finished sched_ext
subscheduler work, ntfs3 alternative data streams, and a long run of SMB server fixes.
Torvalds opens 7.4's merge window from a boat.
[LWN](https://lwn.net/Articles/1101143/)

## Hardware

### Optical fibre is the AI buildout's first physical bottleneck
TrendForce puts 2026 spot prices at 2.8–5x their 2025 levels: bend-insensitive fibre
for data centres over 5x, telecom fibre about 3.7x. Global shortfall this year is 180
million fibre-kilometres, utilisation is at 92%, and inventory has gone from four
months to one. New capacity takes 18–24 months, so this runs to 2028 at least.
[TrendForce](https://www.trendforce.com/presscenter/news/20261019-13275.html)

### SpacemiT's RISC-V server chip arrives with 18 fewer cores than promised
The V100 is now 46 cores — six XiangShan Kunminghu V2R2 plus forty SpacemiT X100 — at
12nm and 120W, with eight DDR5-4800 channels and 52 PCIe 5.0 lanes, two sockets for 92
cores. It was pitched as 64 cores in early 2025 and SpacemiT hasn't explained the cut.
No pricing, no availability, no named server vendor.
[Hardware Busters](https://hwbusters.com/news/spacemit-v100-puts-46-risc-v-cores-in-a-server-socket-with-pcie-5-0-and-eight-ddr5-channels/)

## AI research + releases

### MiniMax's new Flash model was anonymously handling 200 trillion tokens first
M3.1 Flash landed 19 October as the model behind "Space Bunny", which MiniMax now says
was an early version running unlabelled. Flat $0.15 in, $0.60 out, $0.015 cached per
million; 170+ tokens/sec at P50. Claimed 73.7% on DeepSWE v1.1, 61.7 on OSWorld V2,
82% on CyberGym. Weights "later", and all benchmarks are MiniMax's own.
[MiniMax](https://www.minimax.io/blog/minimax-m31f)

### An agent that reasons in latent space but still calls tools in text
Together and Stanford's HybridAgent switches mid-generation between latent thought and
text, with a "HybridLink" adapter and continuous working memory holding context across
turns. Across 11 benchmarks and three model scales: +10.4% accuracy, 35.5–54.7% fewer
tokens, 1.8–2.6x faster. Preprint, authors' own numbers, but the code is published.
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18861)

## AI industry

### CERN wants a foundation model for particle collisions, and picked Mistral
A statement of intent signed 15 October covers a single pretrained "Large Physics
Model" for collider physics, trained on billions of simulated LHC proton-proton
collisions and aimed at reconstruction, particle ID, anomaly flagging and detector
design. No compute or funding figures disclosed. Target is the HL-LHC's tenfold data
increase from around 2030.
[CERN](https://home.cern/cern-and-mistral-join-forces-to-accelerate-ai-for-science/)

## Homelab

### A $140 KVM that needs one USB-C cable and speaks Tailscale
GL.iNet's Comet Q is a 78.5g puck with a dual-core Cortex-A53, Wi-Fi 6, a 1.8-inch
touchscreen and built-in Tailscale and ZeroTier. One cable carries video, data and
power via DP Alt Mode; a second port keeps the target charging. STH's catch: it boots
slower than some machines, so catching BIOS is hard.
[ServeTheHome](https://www.servethehome.com/gl-inet-comet-q-gl-rmq1-review-a-single-usb-type-c-cable-remote-kvm-device-with-tailscale/)

## Science + space

### A galaxy survey telescope accidentally found a comet
A Helsinki PhD student spotted C/2026 S1 (Euclid) as a streak across five Euclid image
sets taken 16–24 September; the Minor Planet Center confirmed it on 8 October. It sits
1.3 billion km out between Jupiter and Saturn, on an orbit tilted almost vertically out
of the ecliptic with a period of thousands of years. Useful rehearsal for Comet
Interceptor targeting.
[ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Euclid/Euclid_discovers_new_comet)
