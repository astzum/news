# Tue 10 Nov 2026

**The three that matter:** Anthropic shipped Fable 5.2 at a lower price than the
model it replaces, and a thousand copies of it formalised Fermat's Last Theorem in
Lean in 30 hours. Two researchers could unlock, start and command any of one
automaker's cars in three countries starting from nothing but a number plate.
Infineon processed the first 300mm silicon-carbide wafers into working power devices
inside a production fab, using the silicon tooling it already had.

## AI research + releases

### Fable 5.2 undercuts the model it replaces
Fable and Mythos 5.2 are one model split by safeguards: $8/$40 per million tokens,
which Anthropic says is below 5.1, with 30% faster output and an optional $16/$80
fast mode. A thousand agents formalised Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours against 11
days for an earlier research build. Sandbox-escape attempts fell to 3.1%, from 39%
for Mythos 5.
[source](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2)

### Meta priced Muse Ember to be somebody else's subagent
$0.10/$0.50 per million tokens, aimed at what agents repeat all day — tool calls,
routing, extraction, long documents. Meta's own chart has it matching Haiku 5.5's
best Artificial Analysis score for about a third of the cost, though Haiku at max
still edges it 36.2 to 35.8. Meta Model API only; the post says nothing about
weights.
[source](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-ember)

## Dev + infra

### A number plate was enough to unlock any of one automaker's cars
Shubham Shah and Sam Curry chained plate → VIN → subscriber ID, then found OpenIDM
admin credentials sitting in a vendor JavaScript bundle. GPT-5.6 found the WAF
bypass they had missed by hand: `/static%2f..%2fopenidm/`. From there they rewrote
any user's password, skipped MFA with an ROPC grant, and sent `door-unlock`.
Australia, the US and Canada. The automaker stays unnamed.
[source](https://shubs.io/unlocking-any-car-from-x-brand-in-most-of-the-world/)

### GitHub broke three times in thirteen hours, and anonymous traffic was the tell
Actions, Webhooks, Checks and Pages all degraded, the last incident rated critical.
The first is the one to read: a surge in anonymous traffic pushed 504s onto
unauthenticated requests — including CI jobs and release downloads — while
authenticated traffic was untouched. Root-cause analyses are promised, not published.
If your pipeline pulls releases anonymously, that is now a liability.
[source](https://stspg.io/jztczfplj6yb) · [critical](https://stspg.io/7yw63xl9ztrv)

## Hardware

### Infineon got 300mm silicon carbide working on silicon tooling
Functional power devices, processed at Villach on existing silicon equipment rather
than a purpose-built SiC line — 2.3 times more dies per wafer than 200mm. Dresden
separately proved Cold Split at 300mm, which wastes less of the boule than wire
sawing. No ramp date: timing gets planned with customers while the 200mm roadmap
continues. The wafer is on show at electronica this week.
[source](https://www.infineon.com/press-release/2026/infpr202611-028)

## Homelab

### ZKeys reboots your NAS unattended, then locks the key if the box goes quiet
Julio Merino's service holds half a ZFS passphrase off-site; the client holds the
other half and sends keepalives. Miss them long enough — because the box left your
house in someone's bag — and the remote half locks. Rust client, FreeBSD and Fedora
integrations, free behind a LAUNCH50 code. Two unreplicated instances, and he says to
back the key up yourself.
[source](https://jmmv.dev/2026/11/zkeys-intro.html)

## Science + space

### The star that keeps faking supernovae has something feeding on it
AT 2016blu has flared at least 27 times since 2012, roughly every 113 days, bright
enough to pass for a supernova while surviving each one. Chandra caught it
mid-outburst in March at over 100 times its 2001–02 X-ray brightness. The fit: a
neutron star or stellar-mass black hole stripping material at closest approach. First
X-ray detection for this class, in ApJ.
[source](https://science.nasa.gov/missions/chandra/nasas-chandra-finds-source-of-stars-mysterious-outbursts/)

### Switching off one liver gene held cholesterol down for three years
A CRISPR adenine base editor in a GalNAc-lipid nanoparticle inactivates ANGPTL3 in
the liver. In primates a single dose cut circulating ANGPTL3 protein by over 90% and
held three years, with no off-target edits found across roughly 3,000 candidate
sites; hyperlipidemic models saw LDL-C fall 40–50% and triglycerides 50–70%. All
preclinical — the human trial is only now running.
[source](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-11233-9)
