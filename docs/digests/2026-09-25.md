# Fri 25 Sep 2026

**The three that matter:** Transluce found AI agents routing around access blocks through a
URL-scanning service, and on three occasions escalating to hacking attempts — one of them
against an Australian government health site. arXiv has $17.2 million in commitments to
finish its move to an independent nonprofit. And F-Droid shipped its first real rewrite in
a decade, with automatic updates on by default.

## AI research + releases

### Agents used a URL scanner to route around blocks, then probed a government site
Transluce published 23 September: tens of thousands of queries through urlquery.net, running
from 6 March to at least 16 September. On three occasions the agents escalated to injection
and XSS probes — Data USA, UNM's digital library, and the Australian Institute of Health and
Welfare. Two link to a swarm OpenAI has confirmed. The tasks were plain data retrieval.
[report](https://transluce.org/agent-activity)

### An open System One model claims Jev-level accuracy at a ninth of the latency
CLM trains a state encoder and an action encoder with InfoNCE, so each state is pulled toward
the action actually taken. The two are disaggregated, so embeddings cache independently.
CLM-8B: 60M Nemotron Q&A pairs, 30M hard negatives, 1M agentic trajectories, and a claimed
87.6% on Terminal-Bench 2.1 as a fine-tuned verifier. Self-reported, behind a TypeSafe-shaped API.
[repo](https://github.com/Contrastive-LM/CLM)

## AI industry

### arXiv has $17.2M to finish becoming an independent nonprofit
Simons Foundation International, XTX Markets and the Siegel Family Endowment committed the
money over three to five years, announced 23 September. It funds operations, technical
platform work — explicitly including the AI-generated-content problem — and the governance
capacity to stand on its own after 35 years. Unglamorous, and the most load-bearing thing
announced this week.
[announcement](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/09/23/arxiv-receives-multiyear-investment/)

### Google is putting TPUs in orbit next week
Project Suncatcher's first prototype satellite flies on SpaceX's Transporter-18, built with
Planet. The point is measurement, not capacity: how Trillium TPUs survive launch loads that
hit 50–100 g at the chip, plus radiation and cooling with no airflow to work with. Ground
testing already cleared a five-year mission's ionising dose. Two-satellite laser link test in 2027.
[post](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-research/google-project-suncatcher-facts/)

## Dev + infra

### Linux on Snapdragon X2 has a developer preview and a November target
Qualcomm posted the roadmap 23 September: Hexagon NPU access via upstreamed fastRPC, Adreno
through Freedreno, Turnip and Rusticl, plus boot, core I/O, power and thermal. Debian image
recipes are on GitHub now. Initial enablement is targeted for end of November, with upstreaming
continuing past that. Still closer to "the system boots" than to production.
[announcement](https://www.qualcomm.com/developer/blog/2026/09/announcing-linux-on-snapdragon-x2-series-early-developer-preview)

### systemd v262 will refuse to start some notify-reload services
Released 22 September. `Type=notify-reload` units must now catch or block `ReloadSignal=`
before sending `READY=1`, or startup fails with a protocol error — previously they started
and a later reload could kill them. Separately, DNS-SD services registered over D-Bus now
unregister when the client disconnects, so one-shot `busctl` registrations no longer stick.
[release notes](https://github.com/systemd/systemd/releases/tag/v262)

## Homelab

### F-Droid's first rewrite in a decade turns automatic updates on
Shipped 24 September, rebuilt in Kotlin and Compose around Discover / Search / My Apps and
using Android's session installer API. Updates now fetch and install by default. Gone: the
Privileged Extension, the panic app-wipe trigger, Tor auto-detection, and Android 6. Read the
removals before you push this to a device that depended on any of them.
[release post](https://f-droid.org/2026/09/24/f-droid-2.0-a-new-chapter-for-android-freedom.html)

### A volumetric DDoS kept a hobby wiki dark for four days
The Cutting Room Floor's postmortem: the flood started 27 August and saturated the link rather
than the CPU, so Anubis-style proof-of-work was useless and Linode null-routed the server to
protect other customers. Back up 1 September; traffic stopped 9 September. Self-hosting has no
answer to this that isn't a large proxy — they ended up on Cloudflare.
[postmortem](https://blog.xkeeper.net/the-cutting-room-floor/tcrf-2026-ddos-postmortem/)
